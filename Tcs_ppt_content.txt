# **Outcome & Learnings**

## **Performance Evaluation & Key Outcomes**

### **Comprehensive Evaluation**

* Evaluated **2 real-time multilingual speech AI pipelines** over **35 clinical conversations** (English, Hindi & Hinglish) covering **7 cardiovascular healthcare scenarios**, including routine consultations, medication counselling and emergency situations. 
* Conducted **both objective (latency, throughput, WER, CER, RTF)** and **subjective (response quality, empathy, human-likeness and clinical appropriateness)** evaluations to benchmark end-to-end system behaviour. 

---

### **Major Quantitative Findings**

**Pipeline 1 (Whisper + Nemotron)**

* Achieved **~41% lower Unified Processing Latency** (**5.04 s vs 8.62 s**) owing to highly optimized specialized ASR and LLM modules.
* Delivered **~79% higher text generation throughput** (**24.53 vs 13.73 tokens/s**) and **over 4× faster audio ingestion** (**173.5 vs 40.8 audio tokens/s**), enabling significantly faster response generation.
* However, cascaded processing caused **acoustic error propagation**, where medical transcription mistakes directly degraded downstream reasoning (e.g., ECG→ICP, Clopidogrel→"clubby dog rhythm"), leading to clinically unsafe responses in several cases. 

**Pipeline 2 (Gemma Audio)**

* Reduced overall transcription errors with **~23% lower average WER** (**16.7% vs 21.6%**) through joint speech understanding and reasoning.
* Produced noticeably **more empathetic, context-aware and clinically safer responses**, particularly during emergency and medication-related conversations.
* The trade-off was **~75% higher processing latency**, primarily due to multimodal audio encoding and blocking XML-tag based response generation, making Time-To-First-Audio unsuitable for low-latency clinical deployment. 

---

## **Critical Engineering Observations**

* **Pipeline 1:** Faster execution but vulnerable to **cascade failures**, where a single ASR error propagated throughout the reasoning pipeline and compromised clinical accuracy.
* **Pipeline 2:** Better reasoning and emotional interaction, but suffered from **multimodal inference overhead** and **streaming bottlenecks**, delaying speech synthesis despite earlier completion of meaningful response tokens.
* Hindi/Hinglish conversations exposed additional challenges including **medical terminology recognition**, **cross-lingual language switching**, and **response truncation**, highlighting the complexity of multilingual healthcare AI. 

---

## **Optimization Strategies Proposed (Ongoing Work)**

* **Pipeline 1**

  * Medical vocabulary biasing and semantic correction layer between ASR and LLM.
  * Confidence-aware transcript refinement to prevent clinical error propagation.

* **Pipeline 2**

  * Asynchronous token-stream parsing to remove XML/tag blocking and enable real-time TTS streaming.
  * Target TTFA reduction from **~8.8 s to <1.5 s** while preserving multimodal reasoning quality.

---

## **Key Learnings**

* Learnt the practical trade-offs between **modular cascaded architectures** and **native multimodal AI systems**.
* Gained hands-on experience with **speech AI pipelines, multilingual ASR, LLM orchestration, latency profiling, performance benchmarking and subjective AI evaluation**.
* Understood that for healthcare conversational AI, **clinical safety, robustness and response quality are often more critical than raw inference speed**, motivating future research on hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of both approaches.
